About

Why "Island Greens"

Named after the 17th at TPC Sawgrass, the most famous do-or-die hole in golf and the closest thing the sport has to a metaphor for buying equipment you can't return.

Stand on the tee at 17 and the shot is simple to describe and hard to hit: a green surrounded almost entirely by water, no room for a bad decision, no mulligan if you get it wrong. Every golfer who's played it, or watched someone else play it under pressure, knows that feeling.

Buying golf equipment has a smaller version of that same problem. A dozen tour balls or a new wedge is real money, the marketing copy all sounds the same, and once it's out of the box, it's yours. Island Greens exists to close the gap between what a product claims and what independent testing actually shows, so the decision feels less like a blind swing and more like a shot you've already played in your head.

How the guides get built

Each comparison pulls from publicly available launch-monitor data, robot testing, and on-course reviews from outlets that don't sell the equipment they're reviewing. Manufacturer claims are included too, but always labeled as manufacturer claims: a spec sheet number and an independently measured number aren't the same thing, and this site won't blur them together. Where the data is mixed or inconclusive, that gets said plainly rather than papered over with a confident-sounding verdict.
