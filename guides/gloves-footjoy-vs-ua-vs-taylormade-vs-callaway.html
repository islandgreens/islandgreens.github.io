Gloves · Four-Way Comparison + Sizing Guide

FootJoy vs. Under Armour vs. TaylorMade vs. Callaway

The cheapest piece of equipment in the bag, and the one most golfers spend the least time thinking about. Before the brand comparison, the sizing question that quietly wrecks more gloves than any material choice does.

A glove is the one piece of gear that touches your grip on every single swing, and most golfers buy whatever size they've always bought without ever checking if it's actually right. That's a bigger deal than it sounds. An estimated one in five golfers is wearing the wrong shape of glove entirely, not the wrong size within the right shape, the wrong shape. That's what the cadet question is about, and it's worth understanding before any brand comparison matters.

Cadet vs. regular, explained

Regular and cadet aren't overall size categories the way small, medium, and large are. They're proportion categories. A regular glove pairs its palm width to its finger length the way an average hand is shaped, longer fingers relative to a narrower palm. A cadet glove keeps the same size range, small through extra-large, but reshapes the proportions: a wider palm paired with shorter fingers. Same size letter, different hand shape entirely.

FootJoy's own fitting guide lays out the simplest way to check which one you need, and it only takes two measurements. Measure the length of your middle finger, from the base of your palm to the fingertip. Then measure your hand's circumference around the first knuckle, excluding your thumb. If your finger-length number comes out lower than your palm-width number, that's the signal to try a cadet glove in your regular size instead.

There's also a simpler real-world tell. Put on a regular glove in your usual size. If the palm and knuckles feel snug and correct but there's loose material bunching at your fingertips, your hand isn't undersized, it's a different shape. Sizing down usually just trades one problem for another, a tight palm this time, since the width shrinks along with the length. That combination, correct palm and excess fingertip material, is the single clearest sign a cadet glove is worth trying before you assume you've just never found the right brand.

Cadet sizing is worn on the glove hand only, the lead hand for your swing, left for a right-handed golfer and vice versa, same as any golf glove. Most major brands offer cadet sizing in men's only, and rarely in left-handed cadet builds, since the overlap between left-handed golfers and the cadet hand shape is small enough that few brands stock it. If you're left-handed and need cadet proportions, expect a narrower selection and possibly a special order.

Three of these four lean on the same basic idea, premium Cabretta leather (sheepskin, prized in gloves for being thin and soft without sacrificing durability) built around a comfortable closure system. The real technical split is Under Armour going a different direction entirely, a synthetic Iso-Chill panel across the back of the hand specifically for heat management, paired with leather only where grip actually happens, at the palm. That's a legitimate design choice for hot-weather golf, not just a cost-cutting shortcut, though it does mean less leather overall than the other three. Callaway's Griptac is the other genuine point of technical difference here, a chemical treatment baked into the leather itself to increase tackiness by a stated 20%, rather than relying on leather quality and closure design alone the way FootJoy and TaylorMade do.

Island Greens Pick

Take FootJoy StaSof if you want the glove with the longest track record and the least to think about, it's been the most-worn glove on tour for over three decades for a reason. Take Under Armour Iso-Chill if you play often in heat and don't mind replacing gloves more frequently, the cooling panel solves a real problem the leather-only gloves don't address at all. Take TaylorMade Tour Preferred or Callaway Tour Authentic if you want tour-level cabretta at a similar price point to FootJoy with a different closure feel, Callaway specifically if the added tackiness from Griptac sounds like it'd help your grip in humid conditions.

From the bag

I've never tried the TaylorMade or Callaway gloves, so that part of this is data, not experience. But between the two I have worn, it's not close. Under Armour is an affordable option with a relatively nice feel out of the box, but it lacks in build quality, the velcro fell apart on mine rather quickly. FootJoy is king, and one I just can't get away from. Soft, a great fit, and it simply lasts. If you've never tried both back to back, the durability gap is the thing that'll actually change your mind, not the initial feel.
