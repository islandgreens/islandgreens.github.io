Golf Balls · Three-Way Comparison

TP5x vs. Chrome Tour Triple Diamond vs. Tour B X

Three firm, low-spin tour balls, each the fastest and lowest-spinning model its own brand makes. Same job description, three different engineering answers.

TaylorMade, Callaway, and Bridgestone each build a flagship ball for the same golfer: fast swing speeds who want distance without handing back all their short-game control. TP5x is TaylorMade's firmest five-layer ball. The Chrome Tour Triple Diamond is the newest, lowest-spin member of Callaway's 2026 Chrome Tour lineup. The Tour B X is the firmest, lowest-spinning ball in Bridgestone's four-model Tour B family. All three share a target golfer. None of them get there the same way.

Construction: three different bets

TP5x is built on TaylorMade's five-layer Tri-Fast core, still the only five-piece construction in the premium ball market. The extra layer gives TaylorMade more room to tune ball speed and spin independently through the bag rather than making one trade-off that applies everywhere. The Chrome Tour Triple Diamond takes a four-piece route, anchored by Callaway's Tour Fast Mantle, a stiffer layer designed to act like a spring at impact. Bridgestone goes a third way with a three-piece build powered by VeloSurge, a new-for-2026 core-mantle integration technology built to tighten the connection between core and mantle for a more unified transfer of energy at impact.

None of these approaches is objectively better. They're three different bets on where ball speed should come from: layer count, mantle stiffness, or core-mantle integration.

Compression and feel

Independent testing has placed the Chrome Tour Triple Diamond's compression around 101, among the firmest balls in Callaway's current lineup. The Tour B X sits at the top of Bridgestone's own four-ball Tour B family for compression, and testers consistently describe it as the only one of the four with a distinct, pronounced click off the clubface rather than the softer thud typical of the RX and RXS models. TP5x has long been positioned as the firmer, faster sibling to the standard TP5. All three land in similar territory on paper, but testers describe them differently in hand: the Triple Diamond's strike is often called softer than its compression number implies, TP5x is described as the crispest, most traditionally "tour firm" of the three, and the Tour B X stands out specifically for that audible, felt click at impact.

Compression numbers are a useful shorthand, but they don't map cleanly to spin or feel on their own. Testing has repeatedly shown firmer balls that spin more than softer ones, and vice versa. Treat compression as one data point, not the whole picture.

Spin: off the tee vs. into the green

All three balls are built to keep driver spin down for fast swingers, and all three succeed at it. The more useful question is what happens once you're inside 100 yards. TP5x uses TaylorMade's Dual-Spin cover system specifically to separate those two jobs: low spin off the driver, higher spin on wedges and short irons, controlled independently rather than as one linked trait. The Chrome Tour Triple Diamond leans further into the speed side of that trade-off within Callaway's own lineup, it's the model Callaway points fast swingers toward specifically because it holds spin down through the bag. Bridgestone's approach is its REACTIV iQ cover, which behaves differently depending on impact speed: firm and fast off the driver, then softer and grabbier on slower wedge and chip shots. One independent launch-monitor test measured the Tour B X's driver spin at roughly 1,900 rpm, about 400 rpm below a Titleist Pro V1 used as the benchmark in that same session, alongside a slightly higher launch angle.

If short-game spin kept separate from driver spin is the priority, that's TP5x's specific engineering pitch. If a flat, piercing flight off the tee matters most and you're comfortable generating your own spin with a wedge in hand, the Tour B X is built furthest in that direction of the three. The Chrome Tour Triple Diamond sits between the two: still low-spin and fast, but with Callaway's core work aimed at keeping some feel in the trade.

Island Greens Pick

Take TP5x if wedge control is the priority. The Dual-Spin cover is purpose-built to keep short-game spin high while driver spin stays low. Take the Chrome Tour Triple Diamond if you want Callaway's fastest, flattest ball and you trust your own short game to generate spin without help from the ball. Take the Tour B X if a piercing, low-spin flight off the tee matters more than short-game grab. All three require swing speeds on the faster end to unlock their design; slower swingers will likely get more out of each brand's softer sibling instead.

From the bag

TP5x is the one I keep coming back to. Long off the tee and it holds greens perfectly fine on approach, no tricks needed. Chrome Tour Triple Diamond is close, just a touch shorter for me, and I noticed slightly more spin showing up across every shot type, not just around the green. Tour B X was the surprise: it feels genuinely long off the driver, but into greens it didn't seem to go anywhere, my approach shots were coming up a full club short all day, which I haven't run into with any other ball on this list. If you play it, be ready to club up on approach.

TP5x is currently in play for Collin Morikawa, Tommy Fleetwood, Charley Hull, and Nelly Korda, confirmed consistently across TaylorMade's own tour materials and independent WITB coverage. Rory McIlroy is often assumed to be on TP5x too, but he actually switched to the standard TP5 in 2025, worth knowing if you're chasing his exact setup. Tour B X carries the strongest documented roster of the three: Tiger Woods, Jason Day, Chris Gotterup, Kurt Kitayama, Harry Hall, and Matt Kuchar all play it in competition, confirmed by Bridgestone's own tour materials and independently by Golf Channel, GOLF.com, and Golf Monthly. We couldn't find a specific named player confirmed on Chrome Tour Triple Diamond itself, the players commonly cited with Callaway's Chrome Tour ball, Xander Schauffele and Sam Burns, are on the standard Chrome Tour rather than the Triple Diamond variant covered here, so we're leaving that one out rather than guess.
