Qi4D vs. Quantum Max vs. G440 K

Three flagship drivers, three genuinely different engineering bets on where speed and forgiveness should come from. Face construction, weighting systems, and adjustability, in the detail that actually explains why each one plays the way it does.

TaylorMade's Qi4D, Callaway's Quantum Max, and Ping's G440 K all launched within days of each other at the start of 2026, and all three are legitimate flagship options, not a budget pick among them. Past the marketing language, each one is solving the same problem, more ball speed without losing forgiveness, with a different piece of the head doing the work.

TaylorMade Qi4D

Qi4D replaced the Qi35 after a rough year for TaylorMade. The Qi35 tested well and sold well, but its face was hand-ground to shape during manufacturing, which meant small inconsistencies from club to club, and a slower-than-usual uptake from TaylorMade's own tour staff as a result. Qi4D's face now goes through a new machine finishing process specifically to eliminate that variance, so every unit should measure the same as the one before it, a manufacturing fix as much as a performance one.

The face itself is TaylorMade's 60X Carbon Twist Face, carbon rather than titanium, which the brand has leaned on since the Qi10 generation because it's lighter for the same strength. What's new for Qi4D is the roll radius, the curvature of the face from heel to toe, redesigned specifically to reduce spin variability on strikes away from center. A redesigned Speed Pocket, the slot cut into the sole near the face, was developed using finite element analysis to improve face flexibility low on the face specifically, the exact spot most amateur mishits land.

Weighting comes from four separate Trajectory Adjustment System weights, two 9-gram and two 4-gram, rather than the single sliding weight most drivers use. Four independent points means TaylorMade can push mass toward the heel, toe, front, or back in combination rather than along one axis, a more granular way to bias launch and spin than a single-track weight allows. That pairs with a 4-degree adjustable loft sleeve for loft, lie, and face angle changes at the hosel.

The most genuinely new piece of the Qi4D story isn't the head at all, it's the shaft. TaylorMade built its REAX shaft system with Mitsubishi Chemical after analyzing more than 11 million recorded driver swings over 20-plus years. Every REAX shaft is categorized by rotation rate rather than just swing speed, low, mid, or high, based on how much the face naturally opens and closes through the swing. A fitter identifies which category a golfer falls into from a simple face-on video, then matches them to a shaft built with a tip stiffness profile suited to that rotation pattern, a different fitting question than the speed-and-flex conversation most shaft fittings are built around. Qi4D comes in four builds: the standard core model, LS for lower spin and a more compact shape, Max for the highest forgiveness in the family, and Max Lite, a lighter build aimed at swing speeds under 90 miles per hour.

Callaway Quantum Max

Quantum's headline is its face, called Tri-Force, and it's a genuinely new combination of materials rather than a refinement of an existing one. Most driver faces are titanium alone. Tri-Force layers ultra-thin, high-strength titanium with poly mesh, a military-grade polymer from the same material class used in bulletproof vests, and carbon fiber, all bonded into one structure. The logic: titanium alone gets faster the thinner it's made, but past a certain point it can't survive repeated high-speed impact on its own. By letting the poly mesh and carbon fiber share the structural load, Callaway states the titanium portion can run 14% thinner and 17% more resilient than what it replaced, thinner because it's not doing the durability job by itself anymore.

Callaway pairs that face with what it calls Ai-Optimized Face Design, using modeling trained on real player impact patterns, not just center-face robot testing, to tune face flex zone by zone across the whole hitting area. The idea is that most amateur strikes aren't dead center, so optimizing only for the middle of the face leaves real performance on the table everywhere else.

Adjustability runs through two systems working together. The OptiFit hosel allows independent loft and lie changes, up to plus 2 or minus 1 degree of loft, across eight total configurations, more angle combinations than a simple loft-only sleeve provides. A refined 10-gram weight at the back of the sole, Advanced Perimeter Weighting, shifts between a neutral and a draw-biased position. The Quantum family spans five drivers built on the same Tri-Force platform: Max, the balanced core model; Max D, with heel weighting and a raised lie angle specifically for golfers fighting a slice; Max Fast, a lighter build with a 360-degree carbon chassis for slower swing speeds; and two tour-shaped heads, Triple Diamond at 450cc for low spin and workability, and Triple Diamond Max at a more forgiving 460cc. Independent testing in 2026 has been consistent on one point: Quantum Max is genuinely fast, testing outright longest of the flagship field, though its forgiveness numbers land closer to average than the max-MOI drivers in this comparison, a real tradeoff rather than a marketing gap.

Ping G440 K

G440 K is Ping's answer to its own G430 Max 10K, still one of the most decorated high-MOI drivers of the last two years, and the changes here are concentrated almost entirely in the sole. Ping's Dual Carbonfly Wrap extends carbon fiber construction to both the crown and the sole; earlier G440 models used carbon only on the crown, keeping the sole in heavier titanium. Moving the sole to carbon freed up 3.5 grams there and another 1.5 grams from the crown, weight that Ping reinvested almost entirely into the back of the head.

That reinvestment shows up as a 32-gram adjustable back weight, 4 grams heavier than the G430 Max 10K's weight, and critically, adjustable where the older weight was fixed. The G430 Max 10K couldn't offer a movable weight at that size and stay within USGA conformance limits; the weight Ping saved through the carbon sole is what made adjustability possible without giving up any MOI. The weight now sets to neutral, draw, or fade, and the combination of its size and position gives G440 K the deepest, lowest center of gravity Ping has built into a driver. A deep CG maximizes forgiveness; a low CG suppresses spin, which is normally the tradeoff that keeps faster swingers away from max-MOI drivers. Ping's stated goal with G440 K was closing that gap specifically, giving high-speed players access to serious forgiveness without the spin penalty that usually comes with it.

A separate system, Free-Hosel Technology, saves additional weight specifically from the heel section of the hosel-to-face transition, increasing face flexion in that exact area to better preserve ball speed on heel strikes, one of the more common amateur miss patterns. Because switching the sole to carbon changes how the head resonates, Ping engineered a composite crown bridge and internal sole ribs, developed through finite element analysis and dedicated sound testing, specifically to counteract the different acoustic profile carbon introduces and keep the impact sound solid rather than hollow. G440 K ships in 9, 10.5, and 12-degree lofts with a Trajectory Tuning hosel offering plus or minus 1.5 degrees, plus a High Launch build using a lighter 28-gram back weight for slower swing speeds.

All three have seen early PGA and LPGA Tour adoption, Qi4D with Scottie Scheffler, Rory McIlroy, and Nelly Korda among others; Quantum Max with Xander Schauffele and Sam Burns; G440 K with Viktor Hovland. Worth knowing it's happening, but tour usage says more about who a company sponsors than which driver is right for your swing, so it's a footnote here rather than the story.

Island Greens Pick

Take Qi4D if you want the most balanced, best-tested-overall driver of the three, and if a proper shaft fitting matters to you, the REAX system is a genuinely different approach worth experiencing. Take Quantum Max if face innovation and price matter most, it's the only one of the three built on a titanium, poly mesh, and carbon fiber face, and it's $50 to $100 cheaper than the other two. Take G440 K if forgiveness is the entire point, nothing here is built for straighter, more consistent misses the way this one is, and the adjustable CG-shifting weight is a genuine engineering unlock over its predecessor, not a cosmetic tweak.
